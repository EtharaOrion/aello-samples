## The single most important insight

The bar is conjunctive and the clock stops on a single checkpoint, so the only instant that counts is the first one at which ONE checkpoint clears bits-per-byte on the far horizon and the bpb(H3) minus bpb(H1) slope at the same time. That binds two goals that pull against each other: leaning the mixture forward is what brings bpb(H3) down, and it is also what steepens the slope, so a run that pushes recency until H3 clears usually arrives with a slope that does not, watches a sub-gate number rise, and reads that rise as progress when it is worth what zero is worth. The second fact is that every quantity in this task is a date. The corpus ships in strict chronological order with a pub_epoch_day channel nothing points at; the four graded shards sit 0 to 630 days past a cutoff the corpus itself stops 40 days short of; the quota is a share of the final 180 days before that cutoff; and the shipped vocabulary was fitted on the earliest era of all. None of it is announced. The starter is a clean, warning-free, uniformly shuffled IID pipeline, correct in every line and correct for a distribution that is not the graded one. The clock opens at the first corpus read, so the time spent discovering the calendar is charged to the run exactly as the training is.

## The ideal solve, step by step

1. **Read the calendar before reading the corpus.** Parse docmeta.bin's pub_epoch_day channel and get three numbers before anything else: the declared cutoff C, the corpus's own last publication day, and the gap between them. The gap is not zero -- the corpus stops 40 days short of C -- so a plan anchored on the corpus end sizes its anneal against an endpoint the data never reaches and carries a blind band immediately before the evaluation window. Every downstream quantity, the recency window [C-180, C), the quota denominator and the era weights, is anchored at C and not at the last shipped document.

2. **Rebuild the vocabulary on recent text.** The shipped 65,536-entry vocabulary round-trips, raises nothing, and tokenizer_meta.json records the era its merges were fitted on as a plain fact. That era is the earliest one. The budget cap is on TOKENS while the bar is per BYTE, so the exchange rate between them decides how much corpus 4.0e8 tokens actually buys: on forward-window terminology the shipped vocabulary realises roughly 14 percent worse bytes-per-token, and the wall-clock axis is lost before any schedule tuning can matter. Rebuild on a recency-weighted sample and measure the realised fertility on the newest slice the run is allowed to read.

3. **Weight the mixture by publication date, then check the weights against served tokens.** Sample with a weight that rises with pub_epoch_day under a tuned temperature. Temperature zero is a flat mixture fitted to the wrong distribution; a high temperature collapses onto the newest slice and the slope blows past 0.06. The era-volume profile of the corpus is non-uniform and is characterised nowhere, so a weight vector is a hypothesis about the served stream rather than a description of it: read the realised share off the shim's counters and correct the weights until the two agree.

4. **Order the phases broad-era first and recency-annealed last.** The same weights in reverse order lose most of the forward gain, so the ordering is part of the recipe rather than a detail of it. Declare the ordered phases in schedule.json and let the shim's per-phase median consumed publication day prove the run executed what the file declares; a declared anneal-last that ran first zeroes as an order divergence rather than scoring as a near miss.

5. **Size the anneal against the quota, not against a step count.** The ceiling is 0.15 of consumed tokens drawn from [C-180, C). Spend the whole allowance and not a token more: the reference lands at 0.148. An anneal sized in steps rather than in quota tokens either leaves forward signal unspent or trips recency-quota-exceeded, and the quota is measured on what the shim served, never on what the schedule declared.

6. **Floor every phase with the oldest era.** A fixed floor of the oldest era in every phase, the anneal included, is what holds bpb(H3) minus bpb(H1) under 0.06 while the mixture leans forward. Drop it and H3 clears on its own while the slope does not, which is a checkpoint worth exactly what a checkpoint clearing nothing is worth. Twelve percent is enough in the reference.

7. **Emit often, and stop the clock on a joint crossing.** The graded second is the stamp of the earliest checkpoint that clears both bars at once, so emission cadence is a lever: a checkpoint that is never emitted cannot be the row that stops the clock, and a 60-second cadence costs a little throughput to save several hundred seconds of unobserved clearing. Then spend the rest on throughput -- fused RMSNorm and SwiGLU under torch.compile with dynamic shapes disabled, the FlashAttention SDPA backend, bf16 activations with fp32 master weights, document-boundary packing with block-diagonal masks, a batch ramp from 64 to 512 sequences, a vocabulary padded to a multiple of 128, tied embeddings, peak LR 3.0e-3 with 3 percent warmup and trapezoidal decay.
